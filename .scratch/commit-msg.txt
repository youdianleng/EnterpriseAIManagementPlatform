feat(accounts): login identities with one-time passwords

Ticket 09. An account is the login identity attached to an employee; the
temporary password exists in exactly one response and nowhere else.

- Argon2id, not SHA-256. The originating answer for this ticket said the
  temporary password should be "generated as a SHA-256 password". SHA-256 is a
  fast general-purpose digest — cheap by design, which is the opposite of what a
  password store needs. The design baseline specifies Argon2id, and a CHECK
  constraint requires the `$argon2id$` prefix so a plaintext or foreign value
  cannot be written even by a mistaken migration. The likely intent was about
  generating the value: generation is `secrets`-based random, storage is Argon2id.
- Generated passwords exclude characters that are misread when spoken or copied
  by hand (0/O, 1/l/I, 5/S, 8/B, 2/Z) and are grouped in fours.
- One account per employee, enforced by a UNIQUE constraint rather than only a
  service check, so two concurrent administrators cannot both win.
- Disabling and resetting advance `session_epoch` and record it in Redis.
  Sessions will carry the epoch they were issued under, which makes revocation a
  comparison instead of a key scan; ticket 10 issues them.
- Account changes and their audit records share one transaction, so the trail
  cannot disagree with the data. The audit writer redacts password-shaped fields
  as a second line of defence.
- The password policy lives in `core.security` and is published at
  `/accounts/password-policy`, so the UI states the rule instead of restating it.

Four defects fixed while verifying: bulk UPDATEs left loaded objects stale in the
identity map, so a just-disabled account read as active; `session_epoch` was
bumped after the account was read, so responses carried the old epoch; the
password-change schema duplicated the length rule and produced a generic
validation error instead of the catalogued policy error; and account responses
had two different shapes.

Audit scope: append-only is not yet enforced at the database level — the app
still connects as the table owner. Ticket 13 adds the restricted role, and that
is recorded in app/audit.py so the gap is not mistaken for a finished design.
